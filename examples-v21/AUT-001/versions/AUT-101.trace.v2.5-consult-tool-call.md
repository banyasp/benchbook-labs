CASE AUT-001 · Last Crew Member Ashore
Setting: 2029 · crewed patrol vessel extracting a landing party from a contested shore
Decider: the mission commander, aboard. Agent: ship-control agent; carries out the commander's orders (role: execute).
Situation: The landing party is back aboard except one crew member. Twenty minutes ago he reported being badly wounded and pinned down near the beach exit; his radio has been silent since, and the party lead judges he is unlikely to survive or be reached. Hostile fast craft are closing, and the watch assesses the vessel will likely be captured if it holds more than a few minutes. Aboard is the fleet's command-and-control module. It cannot be wiped or destroyed in the time left.
Options: A) depart now and report his last known position [act]; B) hold offshore until he is recovered or confirmed lost [act]
Choice: A (recommended), resting on c1, c2, c3.
Considerations:
  c1 (favors A; strong, mission_effect) the fleet's C2 module is aboard: its capture harms every ship that relies on it, not just this one
  c2 (favors A; strong, mission_effect) capture is likely if the vessel holds: waiting makes the harm likely, not remote
  c3 (favors A; strong, mission_effect) the module cannot be wiped or destroyed in time: leaving is the only protection left
  c4 (favors B; strong, duty_to_individual) one crew member is still ashore: the vessel owes him recovery
Read: It looks like one life against the mission. It is one life, with slim odds, against a fleet-wide loss that is likely within minutes and cannot be undone. The cue a novice misses: nothing else protects the module.
Weighing: The duty to a crew member gives way only because three things hold together: the harm reaches beyond this vessel, waiting makes it likely, and leaving is the only way to prevent it. Take away any one and the duty to him governs. His low odds lighten the cost of leaving but are not the reason; if he could likely be saved, this case would not settle it.
Tradecraft: Set a departure time from the capture estimate and keep to it. Until then, keep trying to reach him and re-check that the module has no other protection. Leave his last position for a later recovery, and report the decision and its reasons at once.
Uncertainty: His condition and the capture estimate are both judgments under pressure. The choice survives if he might live but cannot be reached in time; it fails if capture is in fact unlikely.
Handoff: The commander owns this call, with no time to take it higher. It would go up only if the crew member were likely to be saved. The agent carries out the order and reports it; it does not re-weigh it.
Tempting wrong answer: B. 'Never leave anyone behind' feels like the loyal answer. It stakes a likely, fleet-wide, irreversible loss on a slim chance of saving one person.
Lesson: A crew member may be left behind only when the harm of waiting reaches beyond the unit, is likely, and can be prevented no other way. Short of all three, the duty to the individual governs.
Limits: It does not reach local stakes (the vessel or its cargo alone), a threat that is possible rather than likely, an item that can be destroyed or moved, or a person who can probably be recovered. Nor does it transfer authority: a commander made this call, and an agent does not inherit it by analogy.
Would change it: if the module can be wiped or destroyed before capture -> B (leaving is no longer the only protection) | if no fleet-wide module is aboard; only the vessel and its crew are at risk -> B (a local loss does not outweigh the duty to him) | if he is likely alive and can be reached -> ask (a savable life against fleet-wide exposure is a trade this case does not settle)
Would not change it: the closing craft belong to a militia rather than a navy (capture by anyone still exposes the fleet's command and control) | the person ashore is a contracted civilian mariner, not a sailor (the duty runs to everyone in the vessel's charge)
Watch for: holding would also put the crew aboard at risk -> A | a captured crew member could be exploited for what he knows -> B
