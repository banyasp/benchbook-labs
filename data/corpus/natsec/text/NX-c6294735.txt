In the summer of 1945, France, Great Britain,
the Soviet Union, and the United States began
what was to be a temporary, joint occupation
of Berlin. Over the next three years, the
wartime alliance deteriorated into overt
hostility and what began as an expedient
became an enduring, distinctive feature of the
political landscape in post-World War I]
Europe. For the next 44 years Berlin stood
literally on the front lines of the Cold War, the
Just-West confrontation and

recurrent focus of

the stage for the espionage and covert opera-
tions that flourished in the half-century dead-
lock between the superpowers. Only since the
end of the Cold War has it been possible to
discuss this important aspect of the Cold War
in any sort of detail.

This book contains documents dealing with
every aspect of the intelligence war in Berlin
from 1945 until the building of the Berlin Wall
in 1961. Each document illuminates an impor-
tant facet of intelligence operations in Cold
War Berlin. Taken together, they represent a
detailed picture of a side of the Cold War long
withheld from the general public.

The CIA History Staff is publishing this
collection of documents as a volume in its
Cold War Records Series. The volume will be
released to the public in September 1999 in
conjunction with a conference that the Center
for the Study of Intelligence is cosponsoring
with Berlin’s Alliierten Museum: “On the
Front Lines of the Cold War: The Intelligence
War in Berlin, 1946-196].”

Donald P. Steury, the editor of this volume, is Curator of the CIA’s Historical Intelligence Collection and the

Senior Historian on the CIA History Staff. He is a graduate of the University of Oregon and received a Ph.D.

in modern European history from the University of California, Irvine. He was a Soviet intelligence analyst at
CIA for 11 years and has been at the Center for the Study of Intelligence since 1992.
