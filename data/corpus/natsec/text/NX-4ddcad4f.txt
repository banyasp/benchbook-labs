The mission of the Center for the Study of Intelligence (CSI) is to stimulate within CIA and the Intelligence Com-
munity the constructive discussion of important issues of the day, to expand knowledge of lessons learned from
past experiences, and to increase understanding of the history of the profession. CSI manages the production of the
Intelligence Community journal, Studies in Intelligence, and includes the CIA’s History Staff, CIA’s Lessons Learned
Program, and the CIA Museum. In addition, it houses the Emerging Trends Program, which seeks to identify the
impact of future trends on the work of US intelligence

All statements of fact, opinion, or analysis expressed in Studies in Intelligence and CSI Publications are those of
the Authors. They do not necessarily reflect official positions or views of the Central Intelligence Agency or any
other US government entity, past or present. Nothing in the contents should be construed as asserting or implying
US government endorsement of an article’s factual statements and interpretations.
This report is based on a review of unclassified documents, publicly available oral history interviews, and
interviews with senior policymakers involved in supporting the White House during the administrations of
George H. W. Bush, William Clinton, and George H. Bush. This research was sponsored by the Center for the
Study of Intelligence and conducted by RAND.
