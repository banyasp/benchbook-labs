                   SET THE THEATER: A CALL RESOURCE GUIDE



                                     INTRODUCTION
Combatant commanders have a responsibility to set conditions in their areas favorable to the
conduct of operations, or the execution of operational plans. We typically call these set the
theater (STT) responsibilities. STT is a continuous shaping activity to establish favorable
conditions for the rapid execution of military operations.
The geographic combatant commander has overall responsibility for STT activities, but executes
many of his responsibilities through the Army Service component command (ASCC). Combatant
commanders exercise combatant command (COCOM) authority of their ASCC to accomplish
their mission. This includes STT activities.
Title 10 of the U.S. Code provides the Secretary of the Army with a robust collection of
responsibilities and authorities. Most relate in one way or another to the Army’s role in
organizing, training, and supplying land power to the joint force. The Secretary of the Army
meets his responsibilities by exercising administrative control (ADCON) of the various ASCCs,
otherwise known as Theater Armies (TAs).
The operational focus of the TA is to plan for, tailor, and control Army forces. Its administrative
purpose is to execute the service responsibilities specified in Title 10. During contingency
operations, TAs provide a joint force commander and command post for limited contingency
operations, as well as control the reception, staging, onward movement, and integration of
deploying forces. One additional task of the TA is to set conditions for the employment of land
power. In other words, to set the theater.
Theater Army commanders synchronize continuously across the warfighting functions to set
the theater. The TA contributes to joint command and control by establishing, maintaining, and
defending the communications and network architecture. The theater signal command is the
primary executor of these responsibilities.
The TA executes many remaining STT responsibilities through its theater sustainment command
(TSC). Operations often require construction or upgrade of existing facilities, utilities, and real
estate. General engineering support is vital. The TA identifies bases for logistics, medical
support, protection, and infrastructure development, and keeps estimated requirements to
maintaining access to these areas. They assess the infrastructure in their area of responsibility,
and manage infrastructure development.
Set the theater activities may also include: establishing a distribution network, identifying local
procurement requirements, and setting conditions for medical operations. The protection of high-
value infrastructure, materiel, equipment, and personnel is a critical supporting function.
Effective fires require both deliberate and dynamic targeting. Timely and focused intelligence
supports all functional areas, and every operation involves cyberspace electromagnetic activities.
In all of these areas, STT activities are crucial to success.

This catalog provides references for commanders and staff to enhance their understanding of
the concept and details of setting a theater. The resources listed here include current doctrine,
handbooks, articles and other publications focused on STT. Many of these resources are available
on the Joint Lessons Learned Information System (JLLIS). Authorized users with a JLLIS
account can find these resources by logging on to JLLIS and searching for the Set the Theater
Community of Practice (#3277). Search for the number and look under the CoP tab. Log on to
JLLIS at https://www.jllis.mil/apps/. Additional links are for convenience.



                     CALL Products and Resources
News from the Front, Setting the Theater in the Pacific, Lessons and Best
Practices, January 2018
                    This News from the Front (NFTF) article shows how the U.S. Army
                    Pacific (USARPAC) postured land domain capabilities, particularly
                    those that fall under mission command, to set the theater and contribute
                    to security and stability in the Pacific region. It discusses security
                    cooperation, theater posture, and the role of the ASCC. Available at:
                    https://usacac.army.mil/sites/default/files/publications/17596.pdf




News from the Front: Setting Mission Command in a Geographic Theater,
U.S. Army Pacific, February 2018
                    This NFTF article discusses how USARPAC prepares for mission
                    command. It also shows how USARPAC’s Pacific Pathways exercise
                    enables the commander to rehearse mission command by flowing Army
                    forces in and out of theater. Available at: https://usacac.army.mil/sites/
                    default/files/publications/17634.pdf

News from the Front: Setting the Theater in the Pacific, General Engineering
and Protection Support, March 2018
                   This NFTF article provides a framework to help manage general
                   engineering support and protection requirements and functions. It
                   includes tips, insights, and lessons with the intent to benefit USARPAC
                   but also be helpful toward STT in other areas of responsibility (AORs).
                   The publication has two parts: Part I covers basing, infrastructure, and
                   construction; Part II focuses on critical enabling units, agencies, and
                   functions. Available online (CAC access required) at: https://call2.army.
                   mil/toc.aspx?document=17631




CALL Handbook 18-33: Transition to a Joint Force Headquarters, Planning
Insights for Echelons Above Brigade Formations, July 2018
                  Topics contained in this handbook include: build the command and control
                  architecture, form the joint headquarters, plan and operate in the joint and
                  combined environment, integrate force enablers, and leverage joint,
                  interorganizational, and multinational processes. It contains several
                  sections that discuss STT. Available at: https://usacac.army.mil/sites/
                  default/files/publications/18-33.pdf




CALL Newsletter 15-09: Operation United Assistance Setting the Theater,
Creating Conditions for Success in West Africa, June 2015
                    This newsletter is a collection of articles intended to present lessons
                    from Operation Unified Assistance, the U.S. whole-of-government
                    approach to combatting the Ebola epidemic in Western Africa. Chapter
                    5 is especially helpful when considering U.S. Army Africa’s
                    (USARAF) ability to set its theater. Available at: https://usacac.army.
                    mil/sites/default/files/publications/15-09%20OUA%20Newsletter.pdf

CALL Initial Impressions Report 16-04: Pacific Pathways 15-01, November
2015
                 This initial impressions report focuses on the first operational phase of
                 Pacific Pathways 15-01. The greatest value of Pacific Pathways has been in
                 setting the theater. It has set into motion a wide range of activities to build
                 relationships from tactical to strategic, assuring in-theater presence and
                 access, and broadening strategic communication on both sides of the
                 International Date Line. Available online (CAC access required) at: https://
                 call2.army.mil/toc.aspx?document=7331




CALL Initial Impressions Report 16-11: Pacific Pathways 15-02, March 2016
                 This initial impressions report focuses on the second operational phase of
                 Pacific Pathways 15-02. Pacific Pathways operations support set the theater
                 objectives in an expeditionary fashion without increasing permanently
                 assigned forces to the U.S. Indo-Pacific command (USINDOPACOM) area
                 of responsibility. Available online (CAC access required) at: https://call2.
                 army.mil/toc.aspx?document=7374




CALL Initial Impressions Report 16-21: Pacific Pathways 15-03, July 2016
                 This initial impressions report focuses on the third and final operational
                 phase of Pacific Pathways 15-03. Pacific Pathways provides readiness in
                 the areas of rehearsals, repetition, reconnaissance, and relationships.
                 Conducting multiple iterations of mission command planning; sea and air
                 port mobility; employing units and equipment in and out of multiple
                 countries; gaining detailed understanding of Pacific theater time, space, and
                 terrain; and working with diverse partner nations result in operational-level
                 readiness that cannot be gained during combat training center (CTC)
                 rotations. Available online (CAC access required) at: https://call2.army.mil/
                 toc.aspx?document=7400

                 Doctrinal Products and Resources
FM 3-94: Theater Army, Corps, and Division Operations, 21 APR 2014
                   This field manual (FM) contains a robust discussion of both set the
                   theater and set the joint operations area. Chapter 2 (paragraphs 2-33 to
                   2-38) defines the term set the theater, and describes a range of theater
                   army responsibilities. Although it is sustainment focused, this
                   description includes some communications and cyberspace
                   responsibilities. Available at: https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_
                   pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/fm3_94.pdf




ADRP 3-0: Operations, 06 Oct 2017
                   Chapter 5 (paragraph 5-22) of Army Doctrine Reference Publication
                   (ADRP) 3-0 establishes set the theater requirements for the intelligence
                   warfighting function. It lays out three core tasks: first, establish and
                   build an intelligence architecture; second, build necessary knowledge;
                   and third, support the development of context and building of
                   relationships. Available at: https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/
                   PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1003105




ADRP 4-0: Sustainment, 31 JUL 2012
                  Chapter 2 (paragraphs 2-5, 2-6) of ADRP 4-0 discusses setting the
                  theater as the responsibility of the theater sustainment command.
                  Additionally, it distinguishes between setting the theater and setting the
                  joint operations area (JOA). Available at: https://armypubs.army.mil/
                  ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=102982

ATP 3-93: Theater Army Operations, 26 NOV 2014
                    Chapter 1 of Army Techniques Publication (ATP) 3-93 (paragraphs
                    1-11, 1-19) discusses set the theater, describing it as a function of the
                    theater army. In Chapter 3 (para 3-19), ATP 3-93 connects setting the
                    theater to the theater campaign plan. Additionally, chapters 1 and 5
                    elaborate on activities to set the JOA, a related concept. Available at:
                    https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_
                    ID=104853




FM 3-0: Operations, 06 OCT 2017
                    Chapter 4 of FM 3-0 discusses set the theater, describing it as both a
                    prevent activity that enables the joint force to gain positions of relative
                    advantage prior to future operations (paragraph 4-7), and as a shaping
                    activity (paragraph 3-34). This is the most current doctrine available on
                    STT. Available at: https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/
                    Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1003121

LOGCAP Performance Work Statement, 18 JAN 2019
                Logistics Civil Augmentation Program (LOGCAP) is a performance-
                based program which provides broad, contracted sustainment support to
                geographic combatant command (GCC) joint operations, coalition
                partners, and other federal agencies. LOGCAP addresses all ASCC
                regional and country planning requirements, where a military capability
                is not readily available, in support of setting the theater and theater
                sustainment. LOGCAP planners are placed at the ASCC, TSC, and
                corps with LOGCAP decision authority being at the ASCC. This
                excerpt of the LOGCAP V Performance Work Statement covers
                LOGCAP set the theater capabilities.
