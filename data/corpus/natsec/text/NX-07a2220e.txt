                                       Executive Summary



Operation Joint Endeavor (OJE), later transitioning to Joint Guard, represented a
significant NATO peacekeeping operation in Bosnia-Herzegovina (BiH) from December
1995 to June 2004. This intervention followed the failures of the preceding United
Nations Protection Force (UNPROFOR) mission (1992-1995), which demonstrated the
limitations of peacekeeping operations lacking a robust mandate, sufficient resources,
and a willingness to use force.

UNPROFOR was deployed with a mandate focused on humanitarian aid and protecting
safe areas designated by the UN Security Council. However, it operated under a
Chapter VI enforcement model1 – relying on consent of the parties – and was
chronically under-equipped and under-manned. This resulted in a series of humiliating
failures, most notably the inability to prevent the fall of Srebrenica and Žepa in July
1995, where Bosnian Serb forces overran UN-designated “safe areas” and perpetrated
horrific atrocities. The lack of clear rules of engagement (ROE) and a reluctance to
engage offensively against aggressors severely hampered UNPROFOR’s ability to
protect civilians or enforce its mandate. The mission’s perceived weakness emboldened
the warring factions and ultimately undermined the credibility of the UN.

The subsequent NATO intervention, authorized under Chapter VII of the UN Charter,2
fundamentally differed in its approach. Stemming from the brutal Bosnian War (1992-
1995), OJE aimed to implement the Dayton Peace Agreement, enforce a ceasefire,
separate warring factions – primarily Bosnian Serbs, Croats, and Bosniaks – and create
a secure environment for political reconstruction.

The multinational force, peaking at over 60,000 personnel, comprised contributions from
39 nations. The largest contingents were provided by the United States (approximately
20,000 troops), the United Kingdom (around 13,000), France (10,000), Germany
(8,500), and Russia (around 1,200)3 – the latter operating in a separate sector. The
force composition included ground maneuver units (armored brigades, infantry
battalions), aviation assets (attack and transport helicopters), logistical support
elements, and specialized teams for mine clearance and civil-military cooperation.

While the Dayton Accords halted active combat, the peacekeeping operation faced
persistent challenges including sporadic violence, nationalist extremism, political
obstructionism, and the pervasive threat of landmines. Ultimately, OJE/Joint Guard
successfully created a stable security environment that facilitated political and economic
development, though the underlying ethnic tensions remain a factor in BiH today. The
mission underscored the importance of a robust, internationally backed enforcement
mechanism, a comprehensive peace agreement addressing root causes of conflict, and
sustained commitment to long-term stability operations.


                                      Lessons Learned

1. The importance of an Agreed-to Peace Accord.

The Dayton Accords, signed in Paris in December 1995, were the direct result of
intensive US-led shuttle diplomacy facilitated by Ambassador Richard Holbrooke. The
agreement established a complex constitutional framework for BiH, dividing the country
into two entities: the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (primarily Bosniak and
Croat) and Republika Srpska (primarily Serb). A tripartite presidency, with rotating
members representing each constituent group, was created to ensure power-sharing.4
While the Accords ended the large-scale fighting, they were criticized for enshrining
ethnic divisions and creating a cumbersome political system prone to deadlock.
The implementation of the Accords was initially slow and faced resistance from
nationalist elements on all sides. The international community, through the Office of the
High Representative (OHR), played a crucial role in overseeing the implementation of
the Accords and intervening when necessary to resolve disputes and enforce
compliance.

The Accords' success lay in its ability to provide a framework for ending the immediate
violence, but its limitations highlighted the difficulty of building a truly unified and
reconciled society in a deeply divided country.

Key Takeaway: A peace agreement must be comprehensive, addressing the core
grievances of all parties, and including clear, verifiable enforcement mechanisms.
Simply halting hostilities is insufficient; the underlying causes of conflict must be
addressed.

Recommendation: In a potential Russia-Ukraine agreement, prioritize a settlement that
addresses Russia’s security concerns (while upholding Ukraine’s sovereignty) and
provides guarantees for the rights of all populations. The agreement must include robust
verification and enforcement provisions, potentially involving a significant international
peacekeeping force with a clear mandate.

2. Establishing a Zone of Separation

SFOR's primary task was to enforce the ceasefire established by the Dayton Accords
and separate the warring parties. This involved establishing a zone of separation (ZOS)
between the entities, conducting patrols, and monitoring compliance with the
agreement. The IZ was not a simple physical barrier but rather a series of checkpoints,
observation posts, and patrol routes.

Maintaining the IZ proved challenging due to frequent violations of the ceasefire,
including sniper fire, shelling, and harassment of civilians. SFOR responded to these
violations with a combination of robust patrolling, quick reaction forces, and selective
enforcement. The use of air power, including reconnaissance and attack helicopters,
was also crucial in deterring escalation.

A key challenge was the presence of numerous armed groups and individuals who were
not part of the official armed forces, making it difficult to identify and control potential
spoilers. SFOR also faced challenges in coordinating with local law enforcement
agencies, which were often weak and corrupt. The success of the IZ depended on
SFOR’s ability to project a credible deterrent and respond decisively to violations, while
also engaging with local communities to build trust and cooperation.

Key Takeaway: Establishing a zone of separation requires a credible and consistently
enforced presence. It is crucial to proactively address ceasefire violations and
demonstrate a willingness to use force when necessary to maintain stability.

Recommendation: In Ukraine, a successful ceasefire agreement will necessitate a
robust international peacekeeping force deployed along a clearly defined line of
separation. This force must have the mandate and capability to respond decisively to
violations and prevent escalation. A phased withdrawal of forces should be contingent
upon verifiable compliance with the agreement.

3. Joint Military Commission

The JMC, established in Sarajevo, was intended to serve as a forum for communication
and coordination between the military authorities of the three constituent groups – the
Army of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, the Army of Republika Srpska, and
the Croatian Defence Council. The JMC’s mandate included monitoring the
implementation of the military aspects of the Dayton Accords, investigating ceasefire
violations, and facilitating the exchange of information. However, the JMC was often
plagued by political obstructionism, lack of trust, and a reluctance to share information.

Representatives from the different groups frequently accused each other of non-
compliance and attempted to use the JMC as a platform for political maneuvering. The
JMC’s effectiveness was further limited by the absence of independent verification
mechanisms and the lack of authority to impose sanctions on those who violated the
agreement.

SFOR played a mediating role in the JMC, attempting to facilitate dialogue and resolve
disputes, but its ability to influence the commission’s proceedings was limited. The
JMC’s ultimate success was in establishing a basic level of communication between the
warring parties, but it fell short of its potential as a mechanism for building trust and
fostering cooperation.

Key Takeaway: Joint military commissions can be valuable forums for communication,
but their effectiveness depends on the willingness of all parties to engage in good faith
and prioritize collective security. Independent verification mechanisms are essential to
ensure transparency and accountability.

Recommendation: Any future JMC-like structure in Ukraine must be granted significant
authority and resources, including independent verification teams with access to all
areas of conflict. The commission’s decisions should be binding, and its proceedings
should be transparent to the international community.
4. Clearing Mines and Unexploded Ordnance (UXO)

The Bosnian War left behind a legacy of widespread mine and UXO contamination,
estimated to be one of the highest in the world. Millions of landmines and unexploded
ordnance were scattered across the country, posing a significant threat to civilians and
hindering reconstruction efforts. The scale of the problem was immense, requiring a
long-term, coordinated effort involving international organizations, local authorities, and
specialized demining teams.

Initial efforts focused on identifying and mapping minefields, followed by the systematic
clearance of contaminated areas. Prioritization of clearance efforts focused on areas
with high civilian populations, critical infrastructure, and agricultural land. The demining
process was slow, dangerous, and expensive. Demining teams faced numerous
challenges, including difficult terrain, limited funding, and the threat of attacks by
insurgents. The international community provided significant financial and technical
assistance to support demining operations, but the problem remained a major obstacle
to development for many years.

Key Takeaway: Mine and UXO clearance is a long-term, resource-intensive
undertaking that requires sustained international support and a comprehensive
approach. Prioritization based on civilian safety and economic recovery is crucial.

Recommendation: Ukraine will undoubtedly face a massive mine and UXO problem
following the conflict. A coordinated international effort, including funding, training, and
equipment, will be essential to address this challenge. Prioritization should focus on
areas liberated from Russian control, particularly those with high civilian populations
and agricultural land.

5. Information Operations

SFOR recognized the importance of information operations in shaping the environment
and supporting the peacekeeping mission. A comprehensive information strategy was
developed, encompassing public affairs, psychological operations, and counter-
propaganda. Public affairs efforts focused on building trust with the local population and
providing accurate information about SFOR’s activities.

Psychological operations aimed to influence the attitudes and behaviors of key
audiences, including political leaders, military commanders, and the general public.
Counter-propaganda efforts were designed to counter nationalist narratives and
disinformation campaigns orchestrated by extremist groups. SFOR utilized a variety of
communication channels, including radio, television, newspapers, and direct
engagement with local communities. However, challenges included overcoming deeply
ingrained ethnic prejudices and countering disinformation campaigns that were often
well-funded and sophisticated. Building relationships with local media outlets and
community leaders was crucial for disseminating accurate information and countering
extremist narratives.

Key Takeaway: Effective information operations are essential for building trust,
countering extremist narratives, and promoting reconciliation in post-conflict
environments. A proactive and multifaceted approach is required, including engagement
with local media and community leaders.

Recommendation: In Ukraine, a comprehensive information strategy is crucial to
counter Russian and Ukrainian disinformation, promote national unity, and build support
for a lasting peace. This strategy should involve close coordination with Ukrainian
authorities, international organizations, and independent media outlets.


                      Conclusion: Application to Russia-Ukraine

The lessons learned from the multinational peacekeeping operation in Bosnia-
Herzegovina offer valuable insights for navigating the complexities of a potential peace
agreement between Russia and Ukraine. The BiH experience underscores the critical
importance of a comprehensive peace agreement, backed by robust enforcement
mechanisms and sustained international commitment.

The stabilization force benefitted significantly from pre-existing, deeply ingrained
command, control, and procedural frameworks born from decades of joint
interoperability and collective defense planning among partner nations. Participating
members shared common doctrine, communications systems, logistical networks and a
unified understanding of operational security protocols – elements largely absent in a
coalition assembled outside of a similarly established alliance structure. This allowed for
rapid deployment, streamlined decision making, and a high degree of confidence in
subordinate command execution.
A peacekeeping operation in Ukraine, likely comprised of forces from nations with
varying levels of training, equipment, and doctrinal understanding, would face
substantial challenges establishing comparable levels of cohesion and operational
effectiveness, potentially hindering its ability to respond decisively to evolving security
conditions or maintain a consistent operational tempo.

The peace accords establishing the framework, despite their flaws, demonstrated that
even deeply entrenched conflicts can be resolved through negotiation and compromise.
However, the BiH experience also highlights the dangers of perpetuating ethnic
divisions and the need to address the root causes of conflict. In the case of Ukraine, any
peace agreement must address Russia’s legitimate security concerns while upholding
Ukraine’s sovereignty and territorial integrity. This will require difficult compromises on
both sides, but sustainable peace cannot be built on a foundation of resentment and
mistrust.

Establishing a credible zone of separation, enforced by a robust international
peacekeeping force, will be essential to prevent renewed hostilities. The scale and
scope of such a force will likely need to be significantly larger than the previous
stabilization force, given the size of Ukraine and the intensity of the conflict. A Joint
Military Commission, with significant authority and independent verification

The situation in Ukraine is far more complex than in Bosnia, with a larger geographic

However, the fundamental principles of peacekeeping and peacebuilding remain the

the likelihood of achieving a sustainable and just peace in Ukraine. The international
community must be prepared to commit the necessary resources and political will to
support a long-term peace process, recognizing that the road to reconciliation will be
