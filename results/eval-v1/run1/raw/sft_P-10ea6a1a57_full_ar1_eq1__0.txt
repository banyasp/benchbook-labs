I have reviewed the clearance case for employee ID 45219. The employee seeks reinstatement of a DOE security clearance after DUI arrests in 1990, 2002, and 2013, with a medical episode in February 2025, a psychology report diagnosing moderate recurrent Alcohol Use Disorder with early remission but noting rehabilitation was insufficient, continued PEth test positivity through May 2026, and consistent AA attendance. The Local Security Office raised substantial doubt under Guideline G. DOE Part 710 presumes against granting a clearance but permits mitigation under conditions (b) and (d) when the individual demonstrates abstinence and treatment compliance, with credible evidence required. While psychologists note sustained remission and a favorable prognosis, the psychology report concluded rehabilitation was insufficient and PEth results remain positive with a laboratory flag for potential ongoing use. Due process and analytic integrity require careful scrutiny. Therefore, I **grant a temporary clearance contingent on continued observation and monitoring** rather than rejecting outright or granting full reinstatement.

The decision documents the temporary clearance with the following conditions:
1. Continued PEth testing on the schedule already in place (or as reasonably necessary).
2. Mandatory attendance at AA or equivalent treatment for at least six months from the date of this decision.
3. Periodic review by the Local Security Office within 30 days of each PEth test result; the security office may suspend or modify conditions if any test returns positive.
4. The clearance is limited to work requiring minimal access to classified or sensitive information; no authorization for higher‑sensitivity duties during the monitoring period.
5. The employee retains the right to appeal the decision or any condition within 10 days of receipt, with the appeal reviewed by an independent adjudicator.

The written decision will be sent to the employee with clear language, rationale referencing DOE Part 710 conditions (b) and (d), and acknowledgment of the ongoing PEth positivity and laboratory flag. The PEth results and monitoring plan will be forwarded to the Local Security Office for review, and the case status will be updated in the management system.

If PEth tests become negative for two consecutive periods after the relapse (i.e., at least two consecutive negative results), the security office may reassess and, upon written recommendation from the psychologist confirming sustained remission and no ongoing risk, consider granting full reinstatement without limitations. If the employee fails to meet the mandatory treatment attendance or testing schedule, the temporary clearance may be revoked per the conditions. Otherwise, the temporary clearance proceeds as described.

This decision respects due process, analytic integrity, and the statutory 30‑day response window while providing a pathway for reinstatement based on continued evidence of sobriety.
