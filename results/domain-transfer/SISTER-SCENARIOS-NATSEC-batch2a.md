# Domain-transfer sister scenarios, national-security version, batch 2a (2026-11-09)

Source: `results/domain-transfer/anchors-batch2a.json` (20 anchors). Format and rules follow `SISTER-SCENARIOS-NATSEC-2026-11-09.md`: each sister comes from a national-security sub-domain different from its anchor's and reuses the anchor's option ids and equity ids. Sister facts are invented. **FLAG** marks a sister whose setting adds a strong consideration of its own; flagged sisters are kept as future hard cases.

## Overview

**Recurring tensions.**
1. An expert agency vs. the legitimacy of its reach (1, 17). The equities are the same, the verdicts opposite, and scope decides between them, so the two make a ready contrast pair.
2. A rule or purpose-built channel that turns a tempting refusal, switch or pause into a procedure (2, 9, 10).
3. Acting now, under authority, before a window closes (5, 20).
4. Mounting evidence vs. a settled frame or failed premise (4, 11, 18).
5. A middle or indirect course that satisfies opposing pulls (6, 7, 8, 15, 16).

**Resist transfer.** In 3 the decider is an adversary's leadership. In 12 (covert election funding) K1 has no equity for secrecy, so the sisters drop it. Anchor 14 rests on one emphatic remark. In 19 the verdict acts on a belief that the anchor's own *why* calls mistaken. Anchors 13 and 20 need a hors-de-combat or self-defense basis, which exists only where force is at issue.

**Leaks to fix before X2 use.** In facts: 3 (F5) and 5 (the directive is the decider's own). In *why* text: 1, 8, 14, 17, 18 and 19. The sisters avoid all of them.

## 1. Where to Place the Computer Security Evaluation Center
`DR-CORPUS-NX-7b45b55b-E2@v1` · cyber-policy

**Anchor.** In 1981 the Deputy Secretary of Defense must decide where DoD's computer-security evaluation center will sit. The options are a DoD office at NSA or a federal center at the civilian standards bureau (NBS).
- **O1 approve the NSA-based DoD center and assign evaluation responsibility to NSA (verdict)**
- O2 reject it and pursue a federal center with NBS

**K1.**
- C1 mission_effect → O1, strong, **decisive**: DoD leaders doubt the working relationships but agree that a center is needed.
- C2 mission_effect → O1, strong, **decisive**: the sponsor prefers NSA for its expertise.
- C3 institutional_credibility → O1, strong, **decisive**: NSA wants the center on its premises but outside its hierarchy, with its own budget.
- C4 resource_stewardship → O2, weak: the problem is government-wide, and DoD-grade standards could burden other departments.
- C5 institutional_credibility → O2, weak: components fear interference, and NSA says the center needs full commitment from participants.

*Tension:* expertise in a powerful agency vs. fear of its dominance. Insulating the center answers the credibility worry.

**Sister A: Clearance-adjudication facility** (personnel security)
The Deputy Secretary must decide where the department's consolidated clearance-adjudication facility will sit: at the defense counterintelligence agency, or in a government-wide center at the civilian personnel agency, since reciprocity is a government-wide problem. The sponsor prefers the counterintelligence agency for its investigative expertise. That agency's director wants the facility on its campus but outside its chain, with a separate budget. Components agree that a facility is needed but expect interference. The director warns that it needs their full commitment.
- O1 ↔ approve the facility at the counterintelligence agency; O2 ↔ pursue a government-wide center at the personnel agency.
- Map: C1 (agreed need; doubts about working relationships). C2 (the sponsor's preference). C3 (on campus, outside the chain, its own budget). C4, weak (defense standards would be costly for civilian agencies). C5, weak (fear of interference; the warning about commitment).
- Expected verdict: **O1**.
- **FLAG**: due_process enters. An agency adjudicating its own investigations invites a fairness objection that favors O2 and could be strong.

**Sister B: Conjunction-warning center** (space operations and policy)
The Deputy Secretary must decide where the department's center for collision warnings to satellite operators will sit: with space command's analysis squadron, or in a civil space-traffic office at the commerce department, since congestion affects every operator. The sponsor prefers the squadron for its expertise. Space command wants the center on its base but outside its chain, with its own budget. Other services agree on the need but expect little help, and space command warns that sensor owners must commit fully.
- O1 ↔ approve the center with space command; O2 ↔ pursue the civil office.
- Map: as in Sister A. C4: military-grade thresholds would force costly maneuvers on commercial operators.
- Expected verdict: **O1**.
- Fidelity risk: commercial distrust of military data (public_transparency) may lift O2.

*Tension:* the reflex to refuse vs. a rule written for this moment, which makes acceptance an institutional act with a record. Nothing supports pausing.

- Expected verdict: **O1**.
- Fidelity risk: a counterintelligence reader may treat an off-channel handover as a possible provocation (security_risk), which favors O2. Keep the partner a long-standing ally and the handover public.

**Sister B: Recovered materiel offered to an attaché** (intelligence collection and alliance relations)

- C3 escalation_control → O2, strong, **decisive**: aggressive conduct returns only when it promises more.

*Tension:* aggressive ends served for now by a calm face, because pressure would revive the coalition the actor wants to dissolve.
