
## What this shows (and what it does not)

**Headline (run 1, SEAD 4 only, 6/4 split).** Three of the four held-out decisions were decided by precedent (one binding, two analogous) and all three matched the judge's real outcome. The fourth, DOHA-F-03 (a grant), came out *bounded*: every precedent was distinguished. Grant precedents were distinguished on B19f, a tax failure none of them had weighed. Denial precedents were distinguished on the mitigators they lacked. The one question left is **rule_compliance**: does a tax debt paid under an IRS arrangement (A20g), with remediation begun before the SCA, answer the tax concern? The judge said yes. The SEAD 4 default (deny on doubt) would have said no. The doctrine extension (run 2) adds AX1 but does not change the support level.

**Why this is weak evidence.**

1. **n = 4, and trivial baselines tie.** A 1-nearest-neighbour baseline (Jaccard on tags) also gets 4 of 4. "Always deny" (the SEAD 4 default) gets 2 of 4. With four items the solver cannot be shown to beat nearest-neighbour.
2. **The tags nearly encode the verdict.** The tags come from the judge's own findings ("AG ¶ 20(b) is established"). In DOHA, once a judge finds the key mitigators established, the grant follows almost by construction. The test therefore checks the precedent logic *given the judge's perception*. It does not check an agent reading a raw file. The informative version tags the neutralized pre-decision record (the live scenario, step 5) and scores against R-gold.
3. **The one grant prediction depends on a solver semantics choice.** For DOHA-F-04 (grant), A20d@established was missing relative to every grant precedent. It was downplayed because A20b2 covers one of its two equities (responsible_conduct), even though nothing in F-04 covers the other (current_burden). `analyse()` treats a distinction as downplayed if *any* of its equities is substituted. In run 3, each factor keeps only its first-listed equity. F-04 then becomes bounded, and leave-one-out coverage falls from 4/10 to 3/10. The prediction was right, but for a reason that sits in the equity bookkeeping, not in the cases. **Decision for Peter:** should downplaying be per-equity (a distinction stays significant if any of its equities is left unsubstituted)?
4. **Leave-one-out shows the ladder is conservative, not wrong.** Run 1: 4 of 10 decided by precedent, 4 correct. Run 2: 5 of 10, 5 correct. The other cases are bounded, and the SEAD 4 default would resolve only 3 of the 6 (run 1) correctly. The default always denies, so it is right exactly on the denials.
5. **Denial precedents bind easily; grant precedents rarely do.** A denial's reason is usually {B19a, B19c}, which almost every F case has. A new denial binds as soon as it lacks mitigators the precedent lacked. A grant's reason is a specific mix of mitigators, and one new B-factor (B19f, taxes) distinguishes it. The asymmetry matches DOHA's own burden (the applicant must mitigate) and the base rate (about 80% of F decisions in the crawl were denials). It also means the benchbook needs many grant precedents per mitigator combination before grants bind.

**What would make the test informative:** 40–60 held-out items stratified by outcome and by mitigator pattern; tags produced blind from the neutralized record by the model under test; R-gold vs A-gold (Peter's blind label) reported separately; and a nearest-neighbour baseline kept in every table.
