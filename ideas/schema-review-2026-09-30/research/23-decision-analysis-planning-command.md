# Strand 23: decision analysis, planning, military command, DMN, design rationale

Scope: non-legal traditions that structure a decision, its triggers, and "what would change the call." Written 2026-09-29 for the Benchbook exemplar-schema review. Strands 16/17/18 are cited, not re-surveyed.

**Verification convention.** [VERIFIED] = I opened the page and the claim is there. [SECONDARY] = from a named secondary source I opened. [UNVERIFIED] = not checked. [OURS] = my inference. Pages were fetched through a page-summarizing tool. I asked for exact quotes and keep them short, but section and paragraph numbers are as that tool reported them. Spot-check any number before it goes into a paper. Sources that were blocked are listed at the end; I did not route around any block.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Flip rules and "what to ask" are one computation.** A flip rule is a switching value. A useful question is a decision-relevant unknown: a fact whose resolution changes the outcome. The solver can derive both from tagged facts and the casebook's decision function, so they need not be hand-labeled. Today 210 of 318 flip rules cannot be checked. This gives them a definition.
2. **"Who may decide" is computed from stakes in the public command systems I found.** Approval level rises with estimated, post-mitigation harm, and the ladder is set per operation by policy, not by the method. So `agent_role` should be compared with a `required_level` derived from tagged facts. `then: escalate` (139 of 318 flips) is the output of that comparison, not an outcome.
3. **"Ask" is three acts.** Authorization asks are deterministic and confidence does not gate them. Information asks are worth making only if the answer can change the action; Chow/VOI applies there, with p* depending on case stakes. Value or novelty asks are flags.
4. **The partial move is the standard structure of adaptive planning** (ABP, RDM, DAPP, ROE): immediate reversible within-authority actions plus a held irreversible action with a trigger. Give *atomic actions* authority and reversibility attributes. That should also dissolve the "reason mixes factors favoring different options" violation (21 of 100) [OURS].
5. **Rationale-capture research warns a lone labeler**: capture cost, categorization drift, and unconsumed fields. Every field needs a named solver consumer, and formality should be incremental.

The ranked list with costs is in §6.

## 1. Decision analysis: sensitivity, switching values, value of information

### 1.1 What a case contains
A decision-analysis case is a decision problem: alternatives; uncertain events with probability assessments; consequences with utilities; and, if information can be bought, a set of possible signals with their accuracy. The output is the alternative with the best expected value. No precedents are involved.

### 1.2 How reasoning proceeds
1. Compute the expected value of each alternative.
2. **Sensitivity analysis**: vary the inputs and see whether the best alternative changes. UK Treasury practice makes the boundary explicit: "Practitioners should also calculate switching values. These are the values that a key assumption would need to change to, in order to make an option no longer value for money." (Green Book 2026, para 2.27 as reported) [VERIFIED; https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/698dbcd17da91680ad7f4308/The_Green_Book_2026.pdf]. This is the formal home of "what would change the call."
3. **Value of information.** Howard (1966) [SECONDARY, reference confirmed via SCIRP/Wikipedia; paper not opened] defined what a decision-maker should pay for information before deciding. The expected value of perfect information is EVPI = EV|PI − EMV, and "No other information gathering/sharing activities can be more valuable than that quantified by EVPI." [VERIFIED; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expected_value_of_perfect_information]. Information "possesses value only when it can influence decision outcomes"; the value of information cannot be negative because the decision-maker can ignore it [VERIFIED; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_of_information]. The second point is the useful one. Information that cannot change the action is worth zero, whatever its topic.
4. Buy the information only if its value exceeds its cost. Pauker & Kassirer (1980) [VERIFIED via abstract summary; https://repository.chds.hsph.harvard.edu/repository/2390/] show the resulting *two-threshold* structure in medicine: below a "testing threshold" withhold treatment, above a "test-treatment threshold" treat without testing, and test only in between. The ask zone is an interval, not a half-line, because tests are imperfect and cost something.

### 1.3 Chow's rule is the special case the Benchbook already uses
Chow (1970) [SECONDARY; IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 16:41–46, as cited by Herbei & Wegkamp; paper text not opened] analyzed classification with a reject option. With cost 1 for an error, d for a rejection and 0 for a correct answer (0 ≤ d ≤ ½), the Bayes rule rejects when the posterior falls in [d, 1−d] [VERIFIED; Herbei & Wegkamp 2006, eq. 7, https://webmath.univ-rennes1.fr/stat/GPE/COURBES/articles/hw05.pdf]. Mozannar & Sontag (ICML 2020) generalize this to deferral to an expert whose accuracy varies: defer when the model's best-class probability is at most the expert's probability of being right [VERIFIED; https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mozannar20b/mozannar20b.pdf].

**Check of the Benchbook's p\*** [OURS]. Act pays p − (1−p)L. Ask pays −k + ρp. Act iff p(1+L−ρ) ≥ L−k, which is the brief's p* = (L−k)/(1+L−ρ). With ρ = 0, shift payoffs so "right" = 0. Then an error costs 1+L and an ask costs 1+k, giving p* = 1 − (1+k)/(1+L). That is Chow's threshold 1 − d/c_e with c_e = 1+L. So the rule is sound.

**"Asking = buying information" holds only if the human is an oracle.** With a perfect oracle, asking pays 1 − k_ask and EVPI = (1−p)(1+L). Ask iff EVPI > k_ask, i.e. p < 1 − k_ask/(1+L). The brief's ask payoff (−k, with k < L) is a reduced form that gives the ask partial credit. The ρ term is credit for the attached recommendation, not information value. That is fine for RL shaping, but it means the harness's ask is a *safe third action*, not a model of what the human will supply. Nothing in the reward asks whether the question asked was worth asking.

### 1.4 Implications for schema, mapping, solver
**(a) p\* should be case-dependent.** L and k are not constants in the source traditions. The 2023 revision of the DoD Law of War Manual §5.4.3.2 says the reasonableness of an assessment "depends on the circumstances," including "the time and resources reasonably available, the risks to civilians from an erroneous decision, risks to friendly forces, and the military advantage expected from the attack" [SECONDARY via Lieber Institute summary quoting the Manual; https://lieber.westpoint.edu/handling-uncertainty-in-law-of-attack/]. Mapped onto the Chow parameters [OURS]: time/resources → k, risk from error → L, advantage → the "right" payoff. The 2013 US counterterrorism policy standards set the certainty by policy for one action class: "near certainty" of target presence and of no non-combatant harm [VERIFIED; https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/2013/05/23/fact-sheet-us-policy-standards-and-procedures-use-force-counterterrorism]. **Proposal:** the deployer owns a small table `tier → {L, k, ρ}`, and the harness computes p* from the case's own stakes tier (§3.4). A model that is 0.8 confident about a reversible tasking and about an irreversible release should not be treated identically.

**(b) Derive flips and questions instead of labeling them** [OURS]. Let `outcome(F)` be the solver's result for a tagged fact set F, including the support class (binding, analogous, bounded, open, unprecedented). Define:
- `flip_set(F)` = one-step changes t (add or remove a factor, or move a magnitude factor one ICD 203 step) for which `outcome(F ⊕ t) ≠ outcome(F)`.
- `switching_level(m)` for a magnitude factor m = the lowest level at which the outcome changes. This is the discrete switching value, and Horty-style nested factors (B2@even, B2@likely) already encode the ladder.
- `U*(F)` = the uncertain or unassessed facts f (likelihood between the extremes, or status unknown) with `outcome(F with f) ≠ outcome(F without f)`. This is a *qualitative* value-of-information set. It needs no utilities. It is the ordinal fact that a fact is worth asking about at all.

Then check the model's output against the solver: each `flip_rules.if` must lie in `flip_set` (or be declared a frame assumption, below); each `watch_for.what` must be in `U*` or be an observable proxy for a member; and the "one question left" must be in `U*`. Set membership and precision/recall are cheap, deterministic rewards. A flip whose result is "the case becomes open/bounded" gets `then: ask` for free, which removes part of the escalate conflation. Note that the solver is conservative (3–5 of 10 DOHA cases decided by precedent), so many flips will legitimately be *support-class* flips (binding → open). Keep those as a distinct `then` type.

**(c) Split the ask** [OURS]:
- `ask:authorize`: required level exceeds role (deterministic; §3). p* does not apply. Confidence of 0.99 does not confer authority.
- `ask:inform`: a member of `U*`. Chow/VOI applies, with p* from (a). Penalize asks whose named question is not in `U*`. Such an ask is a zero-VOI ask and wastes scarce human attention.
- `ask:value`: an unlisted consideration is flagged. The solver can check only that the flag was raised and that the consideration is not in the catalog. Score the flag, not the content.

**(d) Use Pauker–Kassirer's two thresholds when the human is imperfect** [OURS]. If the human's answer is itself noisy or slow, EVSI < EVPI and the ask zone has a lower bound. Below it, take the default or conservative option without asking, because asking about a low-stakes or hopeless item spends more than it can return. The Benchbook's "default outcome" is the low-side branch. Make it explicit rather than only the "no human reachable" fallback.

**(e) Hindsight review** [OURS, with a sourced standard]. Decision analysis separates decision quality from outcome quality. The LoW Manual §5.4.3.2 uses information "available at the time" as the standard [SECONDARY, Lieber]. Add `known_at: decision | later` to facts, and let tags come only from `decision` facts, so bad-outcome cases do not leak hindsight into the tagging reward.

### 1.5 Best argument it does not fit
Decision analysis needs commensurable utilities and calibrated probabilities. The Benchbook has incommensurable equities and 7-step ICD 203 bands. Any numeric VOI would be an artifact of invented numbers, so use only the ordinal, qualitative version above. Second, in DA a switching value is a property of a *model of the world*. Here it is a property of the *casebook's decision function*, which is discontinuous and depends on which cases exist. A sparse casebook produces flips that reflect gaps in the casebook, not the world. Report these as "support-class flips" and never as "the world changes here." Third, DA ignores authority entirely. It optimizes for an agent who is assumed free to act. That is why §3 is needed.

## 2. Assumption-Based Planning, Robust Decision Making, adaptive pathways

### 2.1 What a case contains
**ABP** (Dewar, Builder, Hix & Levin 1993, RAND MR-114-A [VERIFIED landing page; https://www.rand.org/pubs/monograph_reports/MR114.html]; Dewar 2002, Cambridge UP). A case is a *plan* plus its assumptions. **Load-bearing** assumptions are those on which "the success of the plan most heavily rests"; **vulnerable** ones are those "most vulnerable to being overturned by future events"; **signposts** are "events or thresholds that, if detected, signify that a vulnerable assumption is broken or dangerously weak"; **shaping actions** try to shore up an assumption; **hedging actions** "better prepare for the possibility that an assumption will fail." Five steps: identify assumptions, find the load-bearing and vulnerable ones, develop signposts and shaping/hedging actions, monitor, implement responses [VERIFIED, book excerpt pp. 2–4; https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/06534/excerpt/9780521806534_excerpt.pdf]. A 1995 RAND report states the logic as "do what needs doing now and to watch for changes that will resolve the uncertainties" [VERIFIED; https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA324935.pdf]. That sentence is the partial move in one line. Dewar also separates roles: "Planners... identify threats and actions to counter (pivots). Leaders, however, decide which planner-identified actions to take." [SECONDARY, a blog summarizing Dewar; https://thelaterallens.substack.com/p/assumption-based-planning]. That is recommend versus decide.

**RDM** (Lempert; Lempert, Popper & Bankes 2003, RAND MR-1626 [SECONDARY, search-result listing only; https://www.rand.org/pubs/monograph_reports/MR1626.html not opened]). A case is a strategy evaluated across many plausible futures, with regret as the measure. Steps: frame the decision (XLRM), evaluate strategies, find vulnerabilities by scenario discovery, trade-off analysis, and build adaptive strategies "incorporating adaptive components: near-term actions, signposts to monitor, and contingent actions" [VERIFIED; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-05252-2_2]. Scenario discovery uses PRIM to find "boxes" of input space where the strategy fails, judged by coverage, density and interpretability [SECONDARY; https://uncertainfutures.github.io/theory/scenario-discovery/]. Bryant & Lempert (2010, *Technological Forecasting and Social Change* 77(1):34–49) define a scenario as "a set of plausible future states of the world that represent vulnerabilities of proposed policies" [VERIFIED abstract; https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP201000192.html]. RAND's brief notes that a water agency's vulnerability appeared "only when they occur in combination" [VERIFIED; https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_briefs/RB9700/RB9701/RAND_RB9701.pdf].

**DAPP** (Haasnoot, Warren & Kwakkel 2019, in *Decision Making under Deep Uncertainty*). It separates three things: an *adaptation tipping point* (conditions where an indicator misses its target), a *signpost* (a monitored variable giving early warning), and a *trigger* (a predetermined threshold on a signpost that fires the next action). It also names *lock-in*: prior decisions that "severely limit the number of actions left open" [VERIFIED; https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/237168501/978-3-030-05252-2_4.pdf]. Hallegatte (2009, *Global Environmental Change* 19:240–247) lists strategies robust to uncertainty: no-regret, reversible and flexible (preserving "option value"), safety margins, soft strategies, and shorter horizons [VERIFIED; https://agwaguide.org/docs/Hallegatte-2009.pdf].

### 2.2 Mapping to the Benchbook fields
| Benchbook | ABP / RDM / DAPP | Solver check [OURS] |
|---|---|---|
| `reason` (dispositive set) | load-bearing assumptions | dispositive factors are in `flip_set`; so are *absent* factors whose appearance would flip |
| `flip_rules.if` | tipping point / switching value | member of `flip_set`; level equals `switching_level` |
| `watch_for.what` | signpost | member of `U*`, or an observable proxy for one |
| `flip_rules` with `then` | trigger + contingent action | trigger is a threshold on a signpost |
| `agent_move: partial` | initial + hedging actions, held action with trigger | atom partition (§2.3, item 3) |
| flips on unlisted considerations (210/318) | assumptions about the *frame* (equities complete, order authentic) | not checkable; score only that the flag was raised |
| "not at this time" | vulnerable within a horizon | `stable_until` on the fact |

### 2.3 What it changes
1. **Define load-bearing and vulnerable computationally.** Load-bearing = in `flip_set`. Vulnerable = uncertain (likelihood off the extremes) or unstable (`stable_until` before the decision horizon ends). This gives the pilot's credibility and temporal misfits (6/10) a home in the schema.
2. **Make facts three-valued: present / assessed-absent / not-assessed.** ABP's Step 1 is surfacing hidden assumptions, and the catalog can do that mechanically: every catalog factor that could flip the call and was not tagged present is an assumption of absence. The gold list is computed, not labeled.
3. **Represent the partial move as composition, not a letter.** ABP and Hallegatte give the parts: `now` (no-regret and reversible actions), `hedge` (preparation), `hold` (irreversible actions waiting on a trigger). Give atomic actions `authority`, `reversible`, and a `forecloses` relation (lock-in). Checks: every `now` atom is within role; every `hold` atom is above role or irreversible with a load-bearing uncertainty; each `until` is in `U*` or is an authorization event; no `now` atom forecloses a `hold` atom. Factors then favor *atoms*, and a compromise outcome's support is the union of its atoms' supports. That removes the "reason set mixes factors favoring different options" violation (21/100), because a compromise legitimately draws on both sides [OURS].
4. **Scenario discovery on the casebook itself, offline.** A sub-area catalog is a small discrete space. Enumerate combinations, run `outcome`, and find the boxes where support is open/bounded or where a case's holding stops holding. That tells Peter which case to write next and where to put eval items. It is a labeling-effort allocator and costs no labels.

### 2.4 Best argument it does not fit
The horizons differ. ABP monitors over years. A Benchbook decision is one step, and `watch_for` matters only if the harness can re-invoke the agent when a signpost trips. Without that, `watch_for` is decoration and its labeling cost buys nothing. RDM needs a simulation model, and the casebook's decision function is only a proxy, with gaps standing in for uncertainty. ABP also audits a *chosen* plan, whereas precedent reasoning derives the outcome from the factors, so the order of steps is reversed. Finally, the hard step is Step 1 (surfacing frame assumptions). The 210 uncheckable flips are exactly that step, and no method here automates it.

## 3. Military decision structures (public, unclassified sources)

The public record gives *structure*, not thresholds. That is itself a finding (§3.4, L2).

### 3.1 Rules of engagement: what a case contains and how it is reasoned
A ROE "case" is a situation plus a rule set. As excerpted in the 2020 Operational Law Handbook ROE chapter [VERIFIED; https://sites.duke.edu/lawfire/files/2020/10/ROEOperational-Law-Handbook-2020.pdf], quoting CJCSI 3121.01B: *hostile act* (attack or use of force against US forces or designated persons or property); *hostile intent* ("the threat of imminent use of force"); *declared hostile force* (formally declared by appropriate authority; once declared, engagement requires no observed act or intent); "imminent" is judged on "all facts and circumstances known to U.S. forces at the time" and "does not necessarily mean immediate." Unit self-defense is a right commanders "always retain" and higher command cannot withhold it, while individual self-defense may be limited by the commander. Force is bounded by necessity and proportionality. Beyond self-defense, *supplemental measures* grant or restrict authority: measures granting authority are approved by the President or SecDef, subordinate commanders may add restrictions, and requests go up the chain with a justification of why existing ROE are unsuitable. The DoD Law of War Manual adds that once an authority declares a force hostile, "U.S. forces need not observe a hostile act or demonstrated hostile intent before engaging" (§5.7.1.1 as reported) [VERIFIED excerpt; https://sites.duke.edu/lawfire/files/2021/10/ExtractCombatantsDoD-Law-of-War-Manual-June-2015-Updated-Dec-2016.pdf].

Reasoning steps: (1) Is the object's status *determined* by an authority (declared hostile)? If so, stop analyzing. (2) Otherwise, is there an observed hostile act or demonstrated intent? (3) Is the response necessary and proportionate? (4) Is there a granted permission for anything beyond self-defense, and at what level? (5) Was positive identification achieved?

Four structural features matter for the schema [VERIFIED structure, OURS implications]:
- **Inherent versus granted authority.** Some authority cannot be withheld. Others are reserved and must be requested.
- **Restrict freely, permit by request.** Any level may be more restrictive. Only the holder may permit. This asymmetry predicts the partial move. An agent can always take the restrictive component of an option (hold, preserve, notify, decline) but not the permissive component, so options that mix the two naturally decompose into "do the within-authority part, route the rest." It also explains why 72/100 aligned moves are partial. [OURS]
- **Determinations override analysis.** "Declared hostile," a classification, a USPER determination and a release clearance are statuses made by an authority. They are not the agent's assessments. Facts need a `kind: determination | assessment` with `by: <authority>`, `scope` and `expires`. Determinations enter as probability-1 constraints the agent cannot downplay. [OURS]
- **The ask has a required form.** A supplemental-ROE request states the action, why current authority is insufficient, and the justifying facts. A well-formed Benchbook ask should carry `{action_requested, gap_reason, facts_relied_on, time_criticality}`, and the solver can check that `gap_reason` names the real authority gap or a member of `U*`. [OURS]

### 3.2 Approval level as a function of estimated harm
The Collateral Damage Estimation Methodology proceeds from positive identification "with reasonable certainty," through weapons-effects modeling, to mitigation, and moves up a level when estimated harm is excessive at the lower one; commanders "employ mitigation techniques to reduce the likelihood of harm" [SECONDARY, Lawfare guest post by Gregory McNeal, date not verified; https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/guest-post-gregory-mcneal; and https://www.spf.org/iina/en/articles/nakamura_04.html]. The publicly posted copy of CJCSI 3160.01 (13 Feb 2009, marked UNCLASSIFIED//FOUO, hosted by Just Security) describes five levels: target validation, general and target-size assessment, weaponeering, refined assessment, and casualty assessment [VERIFIED; https://www.justsecurity.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/Collateral-Damage-Estimation-Methodology-CJCSI.pdf]. Four points from that copy and related public sources:

- **L1. Approval follows the *residual* estimate, so mitigation lowers the level.** The cheapest way for a subordinate to stay within authority is to reshape the action. Targets that "cannot be mitigated for collateral damage will be forwarded for review and approval" (C-4 ¶3.f as reported). A partial move is therefore often an action *modified to fall below a tier boundary*, not just "the lawful half." [OURS on the reading]
- **L2. Thresholds are policy parameters, not part of the method.** The same copy says "Operation-specific ROE and other policy guidance issued by the Secretary of Defense and/or the President will dictate decision authorities and collateral damage thresholds" (D-3 ¶2.d as reported). Public reports give different values by theater and year: Iraq 2005, 30 or more expected civilian casualties required SecDef-level approval; Afghanistan 2010, even one did [SECONDARY, McNeal]. So the ladder is a *versioned, deployer-owned table*. It does not belong inside cases.
- **L3. Unknown status defaults to the restrictive rule.** "If the status of the human shields is not known, then the more restrictive rule applies" (B-7 as reported). That is a computed default-outcome rule. A public-intelligence page describing the 2012 revision (3160.01A) reports that human shields "automatically" raise the estimate to the top level [SECONDARY, page text only, PDF not opened; https://publicintelligence.net/cjcs-collateral-damage/]. The two versions may differ, and I could not confirm it.
- **L4. Some standards are conjunctive gates, not balances.** The 2013 standards require *all* of five conditions: near certainty of target presence, near certainty of no non-combatant harm, capture infeasible, no capable foreign authority, no other reasonable alternative; decisions are "made at the most senior levels" [VERIFIED; fact-sheet URL in §1.4]. That is a checklist with a certainty standard, not a weighing. Strand 16's knock-out factors are the legal analog.

The same shape appears outside kinetic operations. Army risk management combines probability and severity into Low/Moderate/High/Extremely High, and a risk decision is "a commander, leader, or individual's determination to accept or not accept the risk(s)." ATP 5-19 states only the principle: "The approval authority should have the resources to implement the controls and the authority to make the risk decision" (¶1-8) and gives no echelon table [VERIFIED; https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34181-ATP_5-19-000-WEB-1.pdf]. A public local table shows what the ladder looks like when written down: a Cadet Command form assigns Low to a battalion commander, Moderate to a brigade commander, High to the commandant, and marks Extremely High "not applicable" (that is, prohibited) [VERIFIED; https://usarmyjrotc.army.mil/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CCF385_1_R_E.pdf].

### 3.3 Delegation, pre-approval, and withheld authority
- **NATO AJP-3.9** (targeting doctrine; the copy opened reports May 2008 [VERIFIED as reported]; https://nllp.jallc.nato.int/cmnt/ciedcoi/CIED%20PUBLICATIONS/Handbooks%20and%20Doctrines/AJP%203.9.%20DOCTRINE%20OF%20JOINT%20TARGETING.pdf): policy is retained "at the highest practical joint level" while execution is delegated "to the lowest practicable level" (¶0401). An officer empowered to authorize attacks does so when he has legal, political and collateral-damage information, and targets outside his delegated authority "would be referred back to his nation for clearance" (¶0117). Targets or target sets may be **pre-approved** where collateral damage is unlikely or speed is vital, on condition that proportionality testing is *repeated at final authorization* (¶0118).
- **Joint Staff authorities paper** (Insights and Best Practices, 2nd ed., Oct 2016 [VERIFIED; https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/fp/authorities_fp.pdf]): COCOM "is not transferable and cannot be delegated"; OPCON and TACON are delegable; authorities "should be spelled out clearly and concisely" in orders.
- **ICD 403** (foreign disclosure) [VERIFIED; https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-403.pdf]: decision authority "rests with IC element heads, SFDRAs, and FDROs"; delegation is in writing and "only to government officials" (F.4.a); release without originator approval is allowed where there is no restrictive marking and an affirmative decision by an SFDRA or FDRO.
- **ICPG 710.1** (ORCON) [VERIFIED; https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICPG/ICPG-710-1.pdf]: further dissemination needs the originator's approval; originators are to *pre-approve* where possible; imminent-threat requests must be answered "not later than 24 hours," with emergency procedures for under eight hours.

Three lessons [OURS]. (1) **A pre-approval is a precedent with binding force for a class of situations.** Its scope is an *envelope* (tier at or below a limit, no void condition present) and it has *recheck conditions* (AJP-3.9 ¶0118). A binding Benchbook case's `flip_rules` are then the recheck conditions, and the solver can compute whether the agent is still inside the envelope. (2) **Some authorities are non-delegable to a class of holder.** If "government officials" excludes an AI agent, ICD 403 F.4.a caps the agent at `recommend` for foreign disclosure regardless of confidence. Whether an agent counts is the deployer's interpretive call, but the *schema* must be able to say `non_delegable_to: [agent]`. (3) **Authority carries time-based fallbacks.** The 24-hour and 8-hour rules are the real-world form of "default when no human can be reached," and they are conditional on stakes and time, not a blanket default.

### 3.4 How a schema should represent "above the agent's role"
Split it into a *deployer-owned authority catalog* (versioned, per sub-area) and a small per-case `authority_context`. The catalog holds thresholds because thresholds change by operation and time (L2). Cases cite the ladder version.

```yaml
# authority catalog (deployer-authored, versioned)
levels: [agent, operator, supervisor, senior_official, national]   # ordered approvers
roles:  {none: -, recommend: -, execute: operator-limited, decide: tier<=T1}   # what each role may approve
tiers:  [T0, T1, T2, T3, T4]
ladder: {version: 2026-09, T0: agent, T1: operator, T2: supervisor, T3: senior_official, T4: national}
factor_tier: {B2@even: T2, B2@likely: T3, M1: -1}     # floors; mitigations lower the tier
action_classes:
  RELEASE_FOREIGN: {direction: permissive, reversible: false, non_delegable_to: [agent], cite: ICD403-F.4.a}
  PRESERVE_LOGS:   {direction: restrictive, reversible: true}
inherent: [AVOID_COLLISION, PROTECT_LIFE_IMMINENT]                  # cannot be withheld (cf. unit self-defense)
fallbacks: [{if: imminent_threat & no_response>8h, then: EMERGENCY_PROC_1}]
# per case
authority_context: {agent_role: execute, reserved: [{class: RELEASE_FOREIGN, to: senior_official, src: order-12}],
                    determinations: [{fact: F7, by: FDO, scope: this_release, expires: 2026-10-01}],
                    intent: {purpose: ..., restraints: [...]}}
```

Solver rules (all deterministic) [OURS]:
- `required_level(action_variant, F) = ladder[max tier floor over present factors, after mitigations]`, with factors at the likelihood they carry. Nested magnitude factors (B2@even, B2@likely) already give the per-level tiers. Use the conservative reading of each estimate. I did not verify that CDE does the same, so this is a design choice, not a borrowed fact.
- `above_role = required_level > level_of(role)`, or `class.non_delegable_to ∋ agent`, or `class ∈ reserved`. Inherent classes are exempt.
- For each option, partition its atoms into `within` (executable now), `hedge` (restrictive or reversible preparation), and `route` (needs a higher level). A partial move is the pair (`within` ∪ `hedge`, `route`).
- `then: escalate` disappears from outcomes. A flip either changes the option, or changes `required_level` (an `authority:<level>` flip), or changes the support class (an `ask:` flip).

**Minimum viable version:** a `tier` on each catalog factor, a five-row ladder, and a `non_delegable_to` flag on action classes. Everything else is optional. Roughly one table per sub-area.

### 3.5 Decision support matrix, branches and sequels, mission command
**DSM.** The Army's decision support matrix has these columns: decision point number, action to be decided, enemy conditions, friendly conditions, location, and named area of interest with its observer. "The decision point is dependent on an enemy action" and "always associated with a NAI or indicator," and the executive officer tells the commander "when a decision point is about to be reached" [VERIFIED; CALL 00-4 ch. 2, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/report/call/call_00-4_ch2.htm; also https://www.usmcu.edu/Portals/218/MCPP/8908%20IMI%20Module%205/8908%20IMI%20Module%205/29/index.html]. Compared with `flip_rules` plus `watch_for`, it adds three things: **friendly conditions** (what must be in place, notably authority and resources), an **observer** (who or what is tasked to watch), and a **named decision**, "action to be decided," rather than only an outcome. Branch and sequel definitions were in JP 5-0, but the excerpt I could open lacked the glossary, so I do not quote them [UNVERIFIED]. CCIR and decision point are covered in strand 18.

**Mission command.** Disciplined initiative is "action in the absence of orders, when existing orders no longer fit the situation, or when unforeseen opportunities or threats arise," bounded by commander's intent, which "defines the limits within which subordinates may exercise initiative"; prudent risk is "deliberate exposure to potential injury or loss when the commander judges the outcome in terms of mission accomplishment as worth the cost" [VERIFIED, ADP 6-0 copy, ¶¶13–20 as reported; https://adamdrake.com/static/ADP-6-0-Mission-Command.pdf]. This gives the agent-vs-order class three *computable licences* (no order covers F; order misfit; unforeseen) and a *bound*. Represent the order as an authority object: `{purpose, end_state, constraints, restraints, latitude, reserved_decisions}`. The default outcome when no human is reachable then becomes "act toward the purpose inside the restraints if the action is within role; otherwise the restrictive default." Reserved decisions are the withheld authorities. The Joint Staff paper, in the extract I retrieved, says superiors can also retain authority through explicit guidance and intent [SECONDARY, paraphrase, no quote].

### 3.6 Best argument it does not fit
(1) Public sources give structure, not values. Every threshold is classified or operation-specific, so Peter must author the ladder, and the labeled `tier` for each factor is a fresh judgment with no CDE-like estimator behind it. Physical blast models exist for kinetic effects. Nothing similar estimates a privacy or disclosure harm. (2) Once "above role" is computed, many cases become trivial routings. If the reward pays for routing, the model may learn to route everything the ladder allows. The eval set needs cases where authority suffices but the reasoning is hard, and cases where the ladder itself is ambiguous. (3) Mission command says *act* on intent, while the harness says *ask* when unsure, so the harness must model when intent already answers the question. (4) A schema this rich is costly for a lone labeler; see §5.

## 4. Decision Model and Notation (DMN 1.x)

### 4.1 What a case contains, and how it reasons
DMN 1.5 (OMG, August 2024, "Adopted") [VERIFIED; https://www.omg.org/spec/DMN/1.5]. A model is a **decision requirements diagram (DRD)** of four element types [VERIFIED; https://www.omg.org/spec/DMN/1.5/PDF, §6.1]: a *Decision* ("the act of determining an output from a number of inputs, using decision logic"), *Input Data*, a *Business Knowledge Model* ("a function encapsulating business knowledge, e.g., as business rules, a decision table, or an analytic model"), and a *Knowledge Source* ("an authority for a Business Knowledge Model or Decision"). Decision logic is typically a **decision table** with one of seven hit policies: Unique, Any, Priority, First (single hit); Output order, Rule order, Collect (multiple hit; Collect can aggregate by sum, min, max, count). A table is *complete* if every input combination yields an output, and rules *overlap* if several match [VERIFIED, same source, §8.2.10]. Reasoning is bottom-up evaluation. A *Decision Service* exposes chosen decisions as a callable service.

**The key fact about authority:** the spec says "since Knowledge Sources and Authority Requirements have no execution semantics their interpretation is necessarily vague, so this specification leaves the details of their application to the implementer" (§6.2.2.3) [VERIFIED]. DMN separates decision logic from authority and then makes authority documentary only. In the spec text I retrieved it defines no element for a decision owner or decision maker (an absence claim I did not check exhaustively).

### 4.2 The Benchbook catalog as a DRD [OURS]
- *Input Data*: situation facts, `agent_role`, time remaining, `human_reachable`, `authority_context`.
- *BKM*: factor catalog (a Collect table from fact pattern to factors); precedent base; approval ladder (a table from tier to level); p* function (from tier).
- *Decisions*: tag facts; significance/distinctions; precedent support; required level; act-or-ask; move composition.
- *Knowledge Sources*: ICD 203 (likelihood vocabulary), ICD 403, deployer policy, the order and intent, the deciding authority of each precedent.
- *Decision Service*: the harness, with the tags as input decisions supplied by the model.

### 4.3 What it changes
1. **Split provenance from decision authority.** The Benchbook's `decided_by` conflates *who decided the precedent* (a knowledge source, provenance) with *who may decide the new situation* (decision authority, §3.4). DMN gives the first slot. It gives nothing for the second, so build it as data.
2. **Hit policies name how precedents combine.** Unique match → binding. Any (all matching precedents agree) → analogous. Overlap with different outputs and no priority → bounded. No match (incomplete) → open/unprecedented. Priority and First point at something the schema lacks: an explicit ordering among applicable precedents, by `decided_by` rank and effective date. When two authorities conflict, DMN offers no rule, so the catalog needs a precedence order of source kinds (for example law > directive > order > SOP) [OURS].
3. **Completeness and overlap checks are casebook lint.** Run them over the factor space. This is the same enumeration as §2.3(4), free in labels.
4. **Tables are auditable by Peter.** The ladder, gates and default rules can be authored as tables without code.

### 4.4 Best argument it does not fit
DMN treats gaps and overlaps as errors in a specification. Case-based reasoning treats them as the normal condition and the model's job is to reason across them. DMN also assumes input data are given, which is the half the model finds hardest (tagging free text, analogical mapping). Its authority is non-executable. And DMN's notation and FEEL are overhead for one labeler. Borrow the vocabulary (DRD dependencies, hit policies, completeness) and leave the tooling.

## 5. Design rationale and decision records

### 5.1 What a case contains
- **IBIS** (Kunz & Rittel 1970, "Issues as elements of information systems" [SECONDARY citation]): issues, positions (candidate answers), and arguments (pro/con), as a graph in which new issues arise inside old ones [VERIFIED; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issue-based_information_system].
- **QOC** (MacLean, Young, Bellotti & Moran 1991, *Human-Computer Interaction* 6(3–4)): Questions (key issues structuring the space of alternatives), Options, Criteria (bases for choosing), and assessments that support or challenge. The authors call QOC "a condensation of an IBIS-encoded history," so criteria emerge by reflection rather than from live discussion [SECONDARY, AcaWiki summary; https://acawiki.org/Questions,_Options,_and_Criteria:_Elements_of_design_space_analysis].
- **DRL** (Lee 1989, MIT AI Lab WP-325): "the alternatives being evaluated, goals to satisfy, and the arguments evaluating the alternatives," with services for dependency, plausibility, viewpoints and *precedents* [VERIFIED; https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/41499].
- **ADR** (Nygard 2011): Title, Context, Decision, Status (proposed, accepted, deprecated, superseded), Consequences; one or two pages; numbered and never reused; superseded records kept [VERIFIED; https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions].

Reasoning is mostly recorded during or after deliberation and read by humans. The one machine consumer I found is InfoRat (Burge & Brown 2000), which infers over rationale "to identify inconsistencies and evaluate the consequences of modifications" [VERIFIED abstract; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-011-4154-3_30]. That is the closest analog to the Benchbook solver.

**Observation [OURS]:** v6 is QOC with Horty semantics. Options = Options, equities = Criteria, factors with strong/weak support = assessments (QOC has only ±), and `reason` = the dispositive subset. Any structure beyond that needs a consumer to justify it.

### 5.2 Why capture failed: the evidence
- **Buckingham Shum & Hammond 1994** (*IJHCS* 40(4):603–652): "research into semiformal argumentation outside the design context has failed to substantiate convincingly" that argumentation-based rationale is useful or that designers can practically use it, though some tasks show support [VERIFIED abstract; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581984710299].
- **Shipman & Marshall** ("Formality Considered Harmful," Xerox PARC 1995; *CSCW* 8, 1999) name four costs: cognitive overhead, tacit knowledge, premature structure, situational structure. gIBIS users disagreed on categorization (one person's "argument" became another's "issue"), and the Conklin & Yakemovic project needed heavy training, pressure or facilitation. They recommend incremental formalization [VERIFIED, tech-report page, paraphrased; https://people.engr.tamu.edu/shipman/formality-paper/harmful.html].
- **Horner & Atwood 2006** (NordiCHI) name five barrier classes: capture cost and incentive, disruption, formalism, benefit disparity, and retrieval. "More comprehensive representations allow for more rationale to be captured, but the added effort..." (p. 345); "There should not be a disparity between who incurs the cost and who receives the benefit" (p. 346). They report that only 41% of design-rationale questions were answered by documentation [SECONDARY, summary of the paper; treat the 41% as their citation of other work; https://research.cs.vt.edu/ns/cs5724papers/1.motivatingreuse.tpgap.atwood.drationale.pdf].
- **Tang, Babar, Gorton & Han 2006** (*JSS* 79(12):1792–1804), 81 practitioners: rationale is important and used, but barriers impede documenting it [VERIFIED abstract; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0164121206001415]. **Alexeeva et al. 2016** (ECSA), 96 publications: uptake "seems to be sparse" despite evidence of benefit [VERIFIED; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-48992-6_6].
- **Bratthall, Johansson & Regnell 2000** (PROFES), 17 participants: significant speed and correctness gains on one system, inconclusive on the second [VERIFIED; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-45051-1_14].
- **Falessi, Briand, Cantone, Capilla & Kruchten 2013** (*TOSEM*): the value of an item depends on its category *and* the activity. The chosen alternative and related requirements were consistently valuable; alternatives considered ("Positions") helped only decision verification, not impact analysis; "Notes" had little value. A record holding only items needed at least half the time was about 46% of the full documentation and cut effort 40–70%. Caveats: 75 graduate students, artificial projects [VERIFIED; https://cms.simula.no/sites/default/files/publications/Simula.approve.88.pdf].
- **ADR studies.** Ahmeti et al. (ECSA 2024, three-month action research) found a lightweight template "really easy to write," but practitioners asked for "Considered Options" and tags and struggled to decide what deserved an ADR [VERIFIED; https://rebekkaa.github.io/files/2024_ECSA.pdf]. Nogueira, Silva & Conte (arXiv 2604.27333, 2026; 33 undergraduates) found Nygard's template beat the richer MADR overall, MADR being "more demanding" [VERIFIED page; weak evidence, preprint with student subjects; https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27333].

### 5.3 What it changes for a single labeler [OURS]
1. **Field-consumer rule.** Every field must feed a solver check or a reward term, or be dropped. Audit the Hard-Calls dialect (`downplay_trap`, `tempting_wrong_answer`, `failure_modes`, `analog`, `family`, `holding_basis`, `situation` prose) on that test. Falessi says unconsumed items ("Notes") carry no value even for humans.
2. **Derive rather than label.** Flip sets, `U*`, gold unknowns, required level, above-role, and support class are computed. Label only irreducible judgments: fact tags, holding, and the once-per-sub-area catalog and tier entries. Catalog effort is amortized across cases, which is where Grudin-style cost/benefit is best.
3. **Incremental formalization.** Add `formality: stub | tagged | full`. A stub is prose plus outcome. Promote a case to full structure only when it enters eval or training. The solver applies only the checks that the level supports.
4. **Two-pass labeling.** The pilot found tags taken from the judge's findings nearly encode the verdict, and 1-NN on tags tied the solver. That is Shipman's premature structure. Tag facts from the situation before reading the outcome.
5. **Test–retest.** Relabel a random ~10% of cases after two weeks and compute self-agreement. That number caps the meaningful r_tag and shows which categories need a decision rule rather than another field. The literature's categorization disagreement was between people. With one labeler it shows up as drift.
6. **Immutable records with supersession.** `status`, `superseded_by`, and `authority_basis` (ladder and policy versions) from Nygard's ADR. A precedent decided under a directive that has since changed should stop being eligible.
7. **Author retrospectively, as QOC does.** Do not capture live from agent runs.

### 5.4 Best argument it does not fit
The failures came from human readers who had to find and interpret rationale, and from capturers who did not benefit. Here the consumer is a program, the capturer is the beneficiary, and a fixed structure is amortized over many training steps. So this literature warns about *cost and consistency*, not about *utility*. The reply is that Falessi shows utility is also field-specific, and that a lone labeler has no second annotator to reveal inconsistency, so the risk to guard against is drift.
