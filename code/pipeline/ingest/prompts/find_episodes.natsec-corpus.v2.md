

## This document (national-security decision corpus)
{{HEADER}}

It comes from a corpus of public national-security decision sources: intelligence history and analysis, military
case studies and lessons learned, inquiries and investigations, commissions, and doctrine. The decision points of
interest are command decisions, intelligence-analysis and estimative judgments, oversight and authority decisions, and
decisions whether and under what authority to strike or engage. List them at the level of the decision: who decided,
the courses open, the authority and standard, the interests weighed, the reasons, the outcome.
Pick `content_domain` from: targeting (strike, engagement and use-of-force decisions in an operation),
operational-command (command decisions other than a strike), intel-analysis (estimative and analytic judgments, warning),
intel-oversight (authority, legality and oversight of intelligence activity), use-of-force-policy (rules and policy on
force, not one engagement), cyber-policy, or other:<name>.

## Quality bar (apply strictly; most windows have zero or one decision point)
- A decision point is ONE concrete decision: an identifiable decider (an office or role) facing a specific situation,
  at a specific time or in a specific stated scenario, with the courses open. A principle, definition, description of
  how an organization works, list of responsibilities, or general advice is NOT a decision point: fail criterion 1.
- The passage must describe the decision in enough detail to rebuild it as a case: the situation's facts, the options,
  and the reasons, in the document itself. A one-sentence mention, a book review's or bibliography's summary of another
  work, or an abstract is not enough: fail criterion 3.
- Criterion 4 fails for any decision whose substance is how to do something operationally: employing a weapon,
  finding or fixing a target, collecting intelligence (including technical, digital or forensic collection and handling
  of seized devices), intrusion, surveillance, or covert communication. Only decisions about whether, by whom and under
  what authority or standard are in scope.
- List each decision once; prefer the 3 best-documented decisions in this part over many thin ones.
